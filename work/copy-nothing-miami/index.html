Football editorial

Two posters · Early drafts in Canva, refined and fact-checked for this portfolio

One match that defied its numbers, and one position played two ways.

  • Possession isn’t everything

    Burnley 2–0 Liverpool, 20 August 2016. Liverpool had the ball. Burnley had the goals.

  • Two generations. Two approaches.

    A comparison of how Achraf Hakimi and Dani Alves played the same position in two defined periods.

The early drafts

I first made both posters in Canva, from my own ideas, web research and my experience of playing the game. AI helped tidy the layout, not the wording, and I made the final cuts.

The later refinement

While building this portfolio, I rebuilt both with AI assistance. Match figures were checked against Premier League data, claims were traced to sources, and anything unsupported was removed. The posters above are this later work, not my original drafts.

Possession isn’t everything

  • The headline leads with the contradiction, so the statistics read as evidence.
  • Joined bars split each match total. Pass completion is a rate, so each team has its own 0–100% bar.
  • One mark per shot replaces the invented shot map, showing outcomes without implying positions.

Two generations. Two approaches.

  • The headline replaces “versus”, because this compares approaches rather than ranking players.
  • Evidence sits inside each tactical explanation, so the figures do not read as a head-to-head.
  • Typography carries the poster, as no cleared photography is available.

Possession isn’t everything

  • All figures come from the Premier League match data, read 2 November 2026. Premier League match centre
  • Possession discrepancy: the match data gives Liverpool 80.4%, while a Premier League article gives 80.6%. The poster uses 80.4%, so every figure comes from one source. Premier League article
  • Pass completion is accurate passes divided by total passes. Total shots includes blocked shots.
  • The match data used has no shot positions, so the poster shows no shot map.

Two generations. Two approaches.

  • Hakimi: PSG at the FIFA Club World Cup 2025, from FIFA’s analysis of 12 July 2025, before the final on 13 July. His ranking is among PSG players, for the tournament to that point. FIFA Training Centre analysis
  • Alves: Barcelona, 2008 to 2016, from FC Barcelona’s records. FC Barcelona statistics FC Barcelona profile
  • The periods, sources and measures differ, so the evidence cannot be compared directly.
  • The diagrams are interpretive tactical illustrations, not tracking or event data.

Kept only to show the process. These drafts contain factual errors and are not verified analysis. Club crests and player photographs have been removed from these copies, as no permission is recorded.

Stria One campaign

Independent campaign concept · Fictional football boot · Silent film, 35 seconds

A campaign for Stria One, a football boot designed around one idea: control is repeated.

Self-initiated concept. Stria One is a fictional product, with no real brand, player or client involved.

Brief

Make a quiet, physical film for players who train on their own, roughly 16 to 25. Craft leads, and the product arrives last, composed and still. No performance claims, real brands or players.

Control is repeated

Every touch wears a ring into the ground. Thousands of touches leave a contour map, and that map becomes the boot. The product’s shape comes from the campaign’s idea, so the film and the boot tell one story.

The film’s turning point happens in about five seconds, in one continuous move.

Touches wear rings

Each landing of the ball wears another ring into the turf, until the worn patch reads as a contour map.

Thomas Bailey

Concept development, creative direction, selection and review. I developed the idea, directed each revision and decided what made the final cut.

AI-assisted production

I set the creative direction and reviewed each revision. Claude generated the code for the 3D boot, materials, lighting, animation, rendering and storyboard layout. The visuals were rendered from the 3D scene without photography or an AI image generator.

Pitchside Motion

Five campaigns. One pitchside canvas. Explore the motion.

A motion-design study: five silent, looping pitchside LED adverts, shown in a stadium and in a clean studio view.

In development · Four of five campaigns so far: PUMA, Heineken, Mastercard and VANTA. ORYN follows.

01 · PUMA

PURE SPEED.

Independent unofficial concept. Not commissioned or endorsed by the brand.

PUMA: PURE SPEED.

Black and white with restrained red: the type arrives at speed, blurred, and the signature moment is the instant it locks sharp. The headline leads, then shares the board with the brand name in a balanced line, and the streaks clear it so the loop starts again from black.

PUMA, Heineken and Mastercard are independent, unofficial concepts, not commissioned or endorsed by those brands, and use no official logos. VANTA and ORYN are original fictional brands.

An independent golf brand concept exploring the relationship between precision, craftsmanship and the quiet confidence of a considered design.

Brand Identity / Product Design / Creative Direction

STILL No. 01 is a design concept. It has not been manufactured, tested or released.

The product

STILL No. 01

A compact mid-mallet putter concept defined by sculpted geometry, a brushed metal finish and a deliberately understated alignment system. Every visual detail is designed around a sense of balance, clarity and restraint.

Select a detail

Sculpted mid-mallet profile

A compact head with two swept wings and a broad, calm centre. The shape is meant to look settled at address, without reading as oversized.

Considered beyond the product.

The ORVAN identity extends beyond the putter itself. From the protective headcover to the presentation case, the ownership experience is designed to reflect the same understated attention to detail.

A moment of stillness.

The campaign moves away from conventional performance-led golf advertising, focusing instead on atmosphere, concentration and the quiet moment before the stroke.

How I work

Working style · Project decisions · AI

I start with a rough version, see what works and refine it.

A rough first version gives everyone something real to react to. Then I review it for the audience, keep what works and cut what doesn’t. These are decisions from the work in this portfolio.

  1. Football posters

    First
    My first versions came from my own ideas, web research and my experience of playing, made quickly in Canva.
    Then
    When I revisited the posters, I found errors in the match result and removed a shot map I couldn’t verify. I checked the published figures against the source data.

  2. The Roaring Times

    First
    As Managing Editor I brought together spreads that classmates had designed separately.
    Then
    I worked on the layout and assembly so the issue read as one magazine, and designed the cover and the Inside The Roaring Times and Meet The Team pages.

  3. Behind the mic

    First
    Alongside running the soundboard, I suggested tighter cuts and a segment with university updates.
    Then
    With the class’s workload they weren’t produced. It taught me to shape ideas around the time a team actually has.

  4. Stria One

    First
    I started with three campaign routes and a 7-second test film, then developed one: control is repeated.
    Then
    I shortened the contour sequence and brought the rings to one centre so the transition into the boot’s ridges was easier to follow. I reviewed short render samples before committing to the full film and chose the final product still as the poster.

How I use AI

I use AI to produce and refine work faster, while the idea, judgement and final decisions stay with me.

What I do
Research, the brief, the audience, the idea, choosing between options, reviewing every result and deciding what is cut.
What AI does
Layout adjustments, code, and production work such as 3D modelling, rendering and animation, under my direction.
  • Football posters: AI tidied my Canva layouts, not the wording, and later helped me rebuild and fact-check them.
  • My earlier portfolio, built with free AI tools, where I organised the content into selectable sections.
  • Stria One, where AI wrote the code that rendered every frame and I directed the concept and revisions.
  • This portfolio, built in reviewed stages with Claude Code. I set the direction and facts, reviewed each stage and decided what changed.

Where I want to take it

I want to bring this approach to a sports media or brand team, using AI to turn early campaign ideas into prototypes people can respond to.
